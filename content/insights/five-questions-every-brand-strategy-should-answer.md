A brand strategy is often delivered as a long document that few people read twice. The test of a good one is simpler: does it settle the decisions a team faces every week? In our experience, a strategy that can answer five questions clearly is ready to be used.

## 1. Who, precisely, are we for?

"Everyone" is not an audience. A strategy should name the people whose choices matter most to the organization, and describe what they need, what they already believe and what would change their minds.

## 2. What do we want them to think, feel and do?

Perception is the goal of branding, so it should be written down. A short statement of the intended impression gives designers, writers and spokespeople a shared reference point.

## 3. Why should they believe us?

Claims need evidence. List the reasons an audience can trust what you say: your record, your expertise, your process, your people. If a claim cannot be supported, it does not belong in the strategy.

## 4. What makes us different, and does it matter?

Difference is only valuable if your audience cares about it. The most useful positioning statements describe a distinction that is both true and relevant.

## 5. How will we know it is working?

Brand work can and should be measured, whether through awareness, quality of enquiries, consistency of use or the ease with which people describe you. Agree the measures before the work begins.

---

When these five answers are clear, the rest of the brand (the identity, the messaging and the campaigns) has a foundation to stand on.
