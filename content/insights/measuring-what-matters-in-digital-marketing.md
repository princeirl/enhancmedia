Digital marketing is unusually measurable, and that is both its strength and its trap. Every platform reports dozens of figures. Few of them, on their own, tell you whether marketing is helping the organization.

## Start from the objective, not the dashboard

Before choosing what to measure, be clear about what the activity is for. Awareness, enquiries, sales, sign-ups and support are different objectives, and each calls for different measures.

## Separate signals from outcomes

Reach, impressions and follower counts are signals: they show that activity is happening. Enquiries, conversions and revenue are outcomes: they show whether the activity is working. Both are useful, but they should never be confused.

## Choose a small number of measures

A report with forty metrics is rarely read and almost never acted upon. Three to five carefully chosen measures, tracked consistently over time, are far more valuable.

## Look at quality, not only quantity

A hundred enquiries that never progress are worth less than ten that do. Wherever possible, follow results beyond the first click, and ask how many enquiries were relevant, and how many became conversations.

## Make every report lead to a decision

The purpose of measurement is to decide what to do next. A good report ends with a short list of changes: what to continue, what to stop and what to test.

---

Measured this way, digital marketing becomes easier to explain, easier to improve and easier to justify.
